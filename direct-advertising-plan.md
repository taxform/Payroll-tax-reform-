## 2. What small niche finance / B2B sites actually charge

All ranges below are from public sources, cited. They are **benchmarks, not this site's prices** — this site is at the low end of every one of them because its traffic is unproven.

### 2.1 Display banners — CPM and flat monthly
- **B2B display CPM, 2025–26:** typically **$5–$18 per 1,000 impressions**, $0.50–$3 per click, with niche / highly specialised audiences pushing past $50 CPM. Source: DemandScience, "B2B Display Advertising Cost" (https://demandscience.com/resources/blog/b2b-display-advertising-cost/).
- **Finance & insurance CPM:** **$20–$50** on programmatic / premium inventory; Meta finance & insurance $14–$18. Sources: Hawke / CPM Calculator Online (https://cpmcalculatoronline.com/), Hawky.ai 2025 benchmarks (https://hawky.ai/blog/what-is-a-good-cpm).
- **Google Display (programmatic, for comparison):** ~$2–$4 CPM. Meta $8–$15, LinkedIn $30–$40+. Source: Hawky.ai, ibid. Direct buys command a premium over programmatic because the audience is targeted and the placement is guaranteed — but only if the publisher can prove the audience.
- **Flat monthly banner rates, small publishers:** **$50–$500/month** depending on traffic (Reacheffect, "How Much Should You Charge for Advertising on Your Website?", https://reacheffect.com/blog/pricing-ads-on-your-website/). A local news site's 2025 rate card: leaderboard 728×90 **$300/month**, skyscraper **$200/month**, tile **$100/month** (The Chronicle, Barton, Vermont, via Bannersnack, https://www.bannersnack.com/blog/how-much-do-banner-ads-and-display-advertising-cost-in-2026). Niche publishers on 2026 rate cards: **$40–$130 CPM** (Science Careers, SAGE Journals) — those are large, established publishers with verified audiences; do not copy those CPMs at this site's traffic.
- **The CPM math that kills a low-traffic CPM model:** at $5 CPM, 1,000 pageviews/month = $5/month. At $15 CPM, 1,000 pageviews = $15/month. **That is why this plan uses flat monthly fees, not CPM, at current traffic.** CPM only becomes the right model once the site can show 10,000+ pageviews/month on a placement.

### 2.2 Featured / sponsored listings (the closest comparable to this site)
- **Inferse (software comparison site, published rate card):** sponsored listing #1 **$149/month**, #3 $79/month, #5 $59/month; featured slot across the site **$149/month**; category/board sponsorship **$199/month** (up to 1,000 board views/month), $399 (up to 5,000), $699 (up to 20,000). Source: https://www.inferse.com/advertise/
- **Other directory / listing sites:** featured listing ~RM99/month (~$21) to RM299/month (~$63), homepage sponsor RM799/month (~$170) — SuperAI sponsor page, https://superai.com.my/sponsor/ (converted at ~RM4.7/$1, indicative only). BerksConnect (local business directory): paid placement from **$99/month**. Source: https://berksconnect.com/blog/for-business-owners/where-to-advertise-berks-county
- **Takeaway for this site:** a featured software listing at **$99–$149/month** is the market-normal starting price for a small comparison site, and it is the placement this site can most credibly sell today — it sits on the page buyers already visit (/software.html), and it does not depend on homepage traffic.

### 2.3 Sponsored articles / sponsored content
- **Small blog, under 5,000 monthly visitors:** **£75–£200 (~$100–$270) per sponsored post.** 5,000–20,000 visitors: £200–£600. 20,000–75,000: £600–£1,800. Source: Lilach Bullock, "What Is Sponsored Content and How Much Should a Blog Charge For It?" (https://www.lilachbullock.com/sponsored-content-blog-pricing/).
- **General blog ranges:** small blog $50–$200/post, medium $200–$500, large / high authority $500–$2,000+. Formula used by several guides: (monthly pageviews ÷ 1,000) × $10–$30 = base sponsored-post price. Source: TabzGM, "How Do I Price My Blog's Ad Space Based on My Traffic?" (https://tabzgm.blogspot.com/2025/06/how-do-i-price-my-blogs-ad-space-based.html).
- **Newsletter:** dedicated send $150–$400 at 20,000–75,000 visitors; $25–$100/month for a newsletter ad on a small local site. Sources: Lilach Bullock, ibid.; BerksConnect, ibid.
- **This site has no newsletter yet.** Do not sell a newsletter sponsorship until a list exists with a real subscriber count. Selling it now would be selling something that does not exist.

### 2.4 What this means for payrolltaxreform.com
- The site cannot credibly charge programmatic CPMs or large-publisher CPMs yet, because it cannot show impressions.
- It **can** charge flat monthly fees for a small number of high-intent placements, because the buyer is paying for *position in front of a buyer*, not for raw volume. That is the Inferse model, and it is the right model here.
- The honest pitch is: "Small, high-intent audience, cheap to test, first sponsors get founding pricing locked in." The dishonest pitch — quoting $40+ CPMs, or claiming thousands of readers — will lose the advertiser on the first monthly report, and will damage the backlink / trust work already in progress.

## 5. Who to target — and who NOT to take

### 5.1 Ideal advertisers (in order)
1. **Payroll software vendors not already on the site as affiliates** — e.g. OnPay, SurePayroll, Rippling, RUN Powered by ADP, Deel. They already pay $150–$1,600 per affiliate sale, so a $99–$149/month flat listing is a cheap test for them. Note: if a vendor is also an affiliate partner, keep the two deals separate in writing — the flat fee buys a labelled listing, not a better review.
2. **CPA / bookkeeping firms** — local and national firms that want employer clients. A state-guide category sponsorship ($99/month per state) fits them exactly: a California CPA sponsoring the California guide.
3. **HR / time-tracking software** — BambooHR, Homebase, Deputy, Clockify. Same buyer, adjacent product.
4. **Business insurance, employer benefits, business banking** — NEXT Insurance, Gusto's benefits partners, Mercury / Novo, etc. High-value employer products, same audience.
5. **Tax software / e-filing for employers** — employer 1099/W-2 filing tools, not individual tax filing (the individual filing market is already covered by the CJ e-file link, which needs fixing — see affiliate-link-scan.md).

### 5.2 Do NOT take — to protect AdSense review and trust
- **Gambling / casino** — the site already has casino banners Dan said to leave for now. Do not sell any *new* gambling placement into this program, and do not put a direct gambling ad on a page that also carries AdSense. Google treats gambling as restricted publisher content.
- **Payday lending, high-interest loans, crypto, "make money online"** — high-risk for AdSense and for reader trust on a finance site.
- **Link sellers, PBNs, SEO agencies buying links** — a paid link that passes PageRank violates Google's link-scheme rules and risks the site's search visibility. All advertiser links in sponsored listings must be `rel="nofollow sponsored"`.
- **Any advertiser that wants a guaranteed ranking, a fake review, or an undisclosed endorsement.** That is a paid endorsement without disclosure — an FTC violation, and it destroys the editorial credibility the backlink campaign is building.

## 6. How Dan gets paid, and the contract terms

### 6.1 Payment — recommended, not set up
- **Flat monthly fee, paid in advance, by card via Stripe.** Dan already uses Stripe for the donation page, so Stripe is the natural route: send a Stripe invoice, or a Stripe Payment Link, for the month / quarter / year. Do not set up a new payment processor for this — Stripe is enough at this size.
- **Stripe fees:** 2.9% + $0.30 per successful card payment (US). On $99/month, Dan keeps ~$95.83. On $149/month, ~$144.38. Do not price below $50/month for a placement — card fees and admin eat micro-prices.
- **Terms to put on the invoice:** monthly, in advance; 3 months upfront = 10% off; 12 months upfront = 20% off, rate locked 12 months; cancel with 30 days' notice, effective at the end of the paid month; no refunds after the placement has gone live; if Dan cannot run the placement (e.g. site outage), the month is extended, not refunded in cash, unless the outage exceeds 7 days.
- **Do not offer pay-per-click or pay-per-sale for direct ads.** That is the affiliate model, and it already exists on /partners.html. Direct ads are flat-fee for position — simpler to sell, simpler to report, and no dispute about what counts as a conversion.

### 6.2 Contract terms to include (one-page order form is enough at this size)
1. **Placement, dates, price, and creative specs** — exact page(s), size, label, start/end dates.
2. **No guarantee** — no guarantee of impressions, clicks, leads, or sales. The monthly report is the deliverable; results are not.
3. **FTC sponsored disclosure** — every paid placement is labelled "Sponsored" or "Advertisement" at the top of the placement, and sponsored articles carry a disclosure at the top of the article. Advertiser links are `rel="nofollow sponsored"`.
4. **AdSense policy compliance** — direct ads must be distinguishable from site content and from Google ads; Dan will not place a direct ad in a way that encourages clicks on Google ads, and will not run a direct ad on a page that exists only to show ads.
5. **Editorial independence** — payment does not buy a ranking, a verdict, or a review, and Dan may refuse or remove an ad that is misleading, illegal, or off-topic, with a pro-rata refund for the unrun period.
6. **Creative approval** — advertiser supplies creative to spec; Dan approves it and may reject creative that is misleading or does not fit the site. Advertiser warrants it has the right to use the creative.
7. **Cancellation** — either side, 30 days' notice, effective at the end of the paid month. No refunds after launch, except pro-rata if Dan removes the ad for a policy reason.
8. **Reporting** — monthly, by email, within 5 business days of month-end: impressions, clicks, CTR, and the pages the ad ran on.

### 6.3 Measurement / reporting to advertisers (once Analytics is active)
- **What to report:** impressions (how many times the ad was shown), clicks, click-through rate (clicks ÷ impressions), and the placement / page. That is it — do not report sessions, users, or revenue unless the advertiser asks and Dan agrees.
- **How to measure it:** GA4 (once the payrolltaxreform.com stream is created and the tag is on the site) for pageviews/sessions, plus UTM parameters on every advertiser link (`?utm_source=payrolltaxreform&utm_medium=display&utm_campaign=[advertiser]-[month]`), plus a simple click count from the site's own logs / the ad placement code. Do not promise real-time dashboards — a monthly email report is the right level at this size.
- **Until Analytics is active, do not promise a report with numbers.** Tell the first sponsors: "We will report impressions and clicks monthly once our Analytics is live; until then we will report the placement and the dates it ran." Selling a report you cannot produce is the fastest way to lose a sponsor.

## 7. How to promote it — without spending money

1. **Publish the revised /advertise.html** (draft in §4.2) and add the footer link in §4.1. That is the whole "promotion" at this traffic level — inbound inquiries from vendors who find the site.
2. **Outreach, not ads:** email the top 3 targets in §8 with the pitch in §4.3, one at a time, personalised. Do not mass-email. Do not buy a list.
3. **Founding sponsor offer:** first 5 advertisers, launch price locked 12 months + a free month on renewal. That is the incentive to try an unproven site.
4. **Do not spend on paid traffic to sell ads.** The site's own traffic is the product — buying traffic to inflate impressions would violate AdSense invalid-traffic rules and would be discovered on the first monthly report.
5. **Review prices every 3 months,** and raise them when GA4 shows a tier has been reached (10k, then 50k sessions/month). Tell existing sponsors 30 days before a price rise; founding sponsors keep their locked rate.
