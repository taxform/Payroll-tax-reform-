How to File W-2s for 2026 (Due February 1, 2027): Employer Checklist

Every employer with W-2 staff faces the same January deadline: one form per employee, two copies to two different places, on the same day — and the date is not January 31 this year. This guide walks through the 2026 W-2 from start to finish: who must file, the February 1, 2027 deadline, the 10-return e-file rule, each box explained, two worked examples, how to reconcile with your four quarterly 941s, and what late filing actually costs.

Quick answer: For tax year 2026, give every employee their W-2 (Copy B) and file Copy A with the Social Security Administration by Monday, February 1, 2027 — the usual January 31 date falls on a Sunday, so the deadline moves to the next business day. If you file 10 or more information returns in the year (W-2s plus 1099s combined), you must e-file through the SSA's Business Services Online portal, which generates Form W-3 for you automatically.

Figures verified November 13, 2026 against the IRS General Instructions for Forms W-2 and W-3 (2026), IRS Topic No. 752, and the IRS information-return penalty schedule for returns due in 2027. The 2026 form includes new reporting under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (enacted July 4, 2025) — check the current 2026 instructions before you file, because the IRS was still updating form guidance when this guide was drafted.

The two deadlines — both February 1, 2027

Unlike most tax filings, the W-2 has two obligations on the same day, to two different recipients:

WhatTo whomDue
Furnish Copy B (plus Copies C and 2)Each employeeFebruary 1, 2027
File Copy A with Form W-3Social Security AdministrationFebruary 1, 2027

The date surprises people because the deadline is usually January 31. For 2026 wages it is February 1, 2027, because January 31, 2027 is a Sunday and the IRS weekend rule (Publication 509) moves a deadline that falls on a weekend or legal holiday to the next business day. The same February 1 date applies whether you file on paper or electronically — e-filing buys you no extra time. Do not send W-2s to the IRS; they go to the SSA, which shares the wage data with the IRS.

Who must file a W-2

You must file a W-2 for every employee to whom you paid wages subject to federal income tax withholding, Social Security tax, or Medicare tax during 2026 — even if the employee worked for you for only part of the year, and even if they have since left. A W-2 is also required when no tax was withheld if the wages paid were $2,000 or more: for 2026 the reporting threshold rose from $600 to $2,000 for wages paid when no federal income, Social Security, or Medicare tax was withheld. Household employers and agricultural employers have their own rules — this guide covers ordinary employers.

  • Former employees count. Anyone who received reportable wages in 2026 gets a W-2, including employees who quit or were terminated during the year.
  • Independent contractors do not. Contractors get a 1099-NEC, not a W-2. If you are unsure whether a worker is an employee or a contractor, that classification question is covered in our W-2 vs. 1099 guide — getting it wrong is far more expensive than a late W-2.
  • One W-2 per employee per EIN. If an employee worked at two locations under the same EIN, they get one W-2 combining the wages. Separate EINs mean separate W-2s.

What's new for the 2026 form

The 2026 W-2 is the most changed in years, because the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (enacted July 4, 2025) added new reporting requirements for tips, overtime, and Trump Accounts. The practical effects for most small employers:

  • New reporting, same deadline. The February 1, 2027 deadline and the e-file rule are unchanged. What changes is what goes in some of the boxes — read the 2026 General Instructions for Forms W-2 and W-3 before you prepare the forms, rather than copying last year's entries.
  • The $2,000 reporting threshold. The wages-paid threshold for filing when no tax was withheld rose from $600 to $2,000 for 2026.
  • Check your software. If your payroll software has not updated for the 2026 form, its W-2 output may be missing the new fields. Confirm the update before your January run — this is the year a stale software version costs you a correction.

The 10-return e-file rule

If you file 10 or more information returns in a calendar year, you must file them electronically. The count is aggregate across nearly all information return types — W-2s, 1099s, 1095s, and others combined — not 10 of one form. Six W-2s plus four 1099-NECs is 10 returns, so all of them must be e-filed. The rule has applied since returns due in 2024 (Treasury Decision 9972); the old 250-per-form-type threshold is gone.

  • W-2s e-file through the SSA, not the IRS — via the SSA's Business Services Online (BSO) portal. You can key W-2s in directly (up to 50 per submission) or upload a wage file. Sign in with a Login.gov or ID.me credential.
  • 1099s e-file through the IRS — from the 2027 filing season, the Information Returns Intake System (IRIS) is the IRS system; the old FIRE system stops taking submissions on November 19, 2026.
  • Below 10 returns, paper is still allowed — but Copy A must be the official red-ink form (or an approved substitute). Forms printed from the IRS website are not scannable and the SSA will reject them, which counts the same as not filing.
  • Hardship waiver: if you meet the threshold but genuinely cannot e-file, request a waiver on Form 8508 at least 45 days before the due date.

How to file, step by step

  1. Close out 2026 payroll first. Run the final 2026 payroll, then check the fourth-quarter Form 941 against your payroll register before you touch the W-2s — the W-2 totals must reconcile to the four 941s (see below), so fix payroll errors now, not in February.
  2. Verify every employee's name and Social Security number. The single most common W-2 error is a name/SSN mismatch. Check against the Social Security card, not the I-9 or your memory. The SSA offers an SSN verification service — use it before you file.
  3. Confirm the wage totals. Box 1 is taxable wages (gross pay minus pre-tax deductions such as 401(k) contributions and Section 125 health premiums). Box 3 is Social Security wages, capped at $184,500 for 2026. Box 5 is Medicare wages — no cap.
  4. File with the SSA. E-file through BSO (W-3 auto-generated), or mail Copy A with a paper Form W-3. E-filing gives you an acknowledgment and catches errors in real time; paper gives you neither.
  5. Furnish copies to employees by February 1, 2027. Copy B for the employee's tax return, Copy C for their records, Copy 2 for their state return. Electronic delivery is allowed only if the employee consents.
  6. Keep copies for four years. Keep Copy A, the W-3, and your payroll records for at least four years after the tax is due or paid, whichever is later.

Worked examples

Example 1 — one employee earning $60,000. No pre-tax deductions, single W-4. Federal figures use the 2026 rates from our 2026 rates reference (Social Security wage base $184,500).

BoxAmountHow it is figured
Box 1 — Wages$60,000Full salary, no pre-tax deductions
Box 3 — Social Security wages$60,000Under the $184,500 cap
Box 4 — Social Security tax$3,7206.2% × $60,000
Box 5 — Medicare wages$60,000No cap
Box 6 — Medicare tax$8701.45% × $60,000
Box 2 — Federal withholdingVariesPer the employee's W-4

The employer matches the employee's Social Security and Medicare withholding dollar for dollar: another $3,720 + $870 on the employer's side, reported on Form 941, not on the W-2.

Example 2 — one employee earning $250,000. This is where the boxes diverge, and where most first-time filers get confused:

BoxAmountHow it is figured
Box 1 — Wages$250,000Full salary
Box 3 — Social Security wages$184,500Capped at the 2026 wage base
Box 4 — Social Security tax$11,4396.2% × $184,500 (maximum)
Box 5 — Medicare wages$250,000No cap
Box 6 — Medicare tax$4,0751.45% × $250,000 = $3,625, plus 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax on the $50,000 over $200,000 = $450

Box 3 lower than Box 1 is correct for a high earner — it is not an error. The Additional Medicare Tax applies only to the employee; the employer does not match the extra 0.9%.

Reconciling W-2s to your four Form 941s

Before you file, add up the four quarterly Forms 941 for 2026 and compare them to the Form W-3 totals. These must match:

  • W-3 Box 1 = sum of Form 941 line 2 (wages, tips, and other compensation) for Q1–Q4.
  • W-3 Box 2 = sum of Form 941 line 3 (federal income tax withheld).
  • W-3 Box 3 = sum of Form 941 line 5a column 1 (taxable Social Security wages).
  • W-3 Box 5 = sum of Form 941 line 5c column 1 (taxable Medicare wages).
  • W-3 Boxes 4 and 6 = the corresponding tax amounts from Form 941 lines 5a and 5c.

The IRS and SSA compare these figures. A mismatch is one of the most common reasons employers receive a CP-2100 or similar notice — and the fix is usually a payroll correction, not a W-2 correction, so reconcile before you file. Our Form 941 guide walks through each 941 line if you need to trace a difference.

What late filing costs

Penalties are per return, for returns due in 2027, and they apply separately to the employee copy and the SSA filing — a single late W-2 can draw two penalties:

When filedPenalty per W-2
Within 30 days of February 1, 2027$60
More than 30 days late, by August 1, 2027$130
After August 1, 2027, or never filed$340
Intentional disregardGreater of $690 or 10% of the amount that should have been reported — no annual cap

Ten employees filed a month late costs $600; the same ten filed in September costs $3,400. The cheapest penalty is the one you never incur — file on time, and if you cannot, file the employee copies first, because employees need them to file their own returns.

No automatic extension. Unlike some business returns, W-2s have no automatic extension. You can request one 30-day extension on Form 8809, but only if you meet the IRS criteria, and the request must reach the IRS before the February 1, 2027 due date. Treat February 1 as fixed and plan backward from it: aim to have W-2s ready by mid-January.

Correcting a W-2: Form W-2c

If you find a mistake after filing — a wrong SSN, a wrong wage amount, a missing Box 12 code — file Form W-2c (Corrected Wage and Tax Statement) for that employee, with Form W-3c as the transmittal. Corrections can be filed electronically through BSO if the original was filed there. Also give the employee a corrected copy, and fix the underlying payroll record so the error does not repeat on the next return. A W-2c filed promptly costs nothing in penalties; the same error discovered by the SSA costs a notice and, potentially, the late-filing penalty tiers above.

5 mistakes that trigger SSA and IRS notices

  1. Name/SSN mismatch. The name on the W-2 must match the name on the employee's Social Security card. A married name that was never updated with the SSA is the classic version of this error.
  2. Filing W-2s with the IRS instead of the SSA. The IRS rejects them, the SSA never receives them, and the clock keeps running. W-2s go to the SSA; 1099s go to the IRS.
  3. Using the wrong wage base. Box 3 stops at $184,500 for 2026. Taxing the full salary of a high earner overstates Social Security tax and breaks the 941 reconciliation.
  4. Forgetting the aggregate 10-return count. Six W-2s plus four 1099s is 10 returns — e-filing is mandatory. Counting only W-2s and filing the rest on paper is a violation.
  5. Skipping the 941 reconciliation. If the W-3 totals do not match the four 941s, one of them is wrong. Find it before you file, not after the notice arrives.

Frequently asked questions

Q: When are W-2s due for 2026?
A: Monday, February 1, 2027 — both the employee copies and the filing of Copy A with the SSA. The usual January 31 date falls on a Sunday in 2027, so the deadline moves to the next business day under the IRS weekend rule.

Q: Do W-2s go to the IRS or the SSA?
A: W-2s go to the Social Security Administration, not the IRS. File Copy A of every W-2 with Form W-3 to the SSA. If you e-file through the SSA's Business Services Online portal, the W-3 is generated automatically and no separate W-3 is needed.

Q: Do I have to e-file W-2s?
A: Yes, if you file 10 or more information returns in the calendar year — counting W-2s, 1099s, and other information returns together, not each type separately. A business with 6 W-2s and 4 1099-NECs has 10 returns and must e-file all of them. The 10-return rule has applied since returns due in 2024.

Q: What is the penalty for filing W-2s late?
A: For returns due in 2027, the penalty is $60 per W-2 if filed within 30 days of the due date, $130 per W-2 if filed more than 30 days late but by August 1, and $340 per W-2 if filed after August 1 or never filed. Intentional disregard costs the greater of $690 per return or 10% of the amount that should have been reported, with no annual cap.

Q: Can I get an extension to file W-2s?
A: There is no automatic extension for W-2s. You can request one 30-day extension using Form 8809, but only if you meet the IRS criteria — the form must be filed before the February 1, 2027 due date. Do not assume an extension will be granted; file on time if you can.

Q: How do I correct a W-2 after filing it?
A: File Form W-2c (Corrected Wage and Tax Statement) for the employee, along with Form W-3c as the transmittal. Corrections can be made electronically through the SSA's Business Services Online portal if the original was filed there. Also give the employee a corrected copy and fix your payroll records so the error does not repeat.

Q: Why is Box 3 lower than Box 1 on a W-2?
A: Box 1 is total taxable wages; Box 3 is Social Security wages, which stop at the annual wage base — $184,500 for 2026. An employee earning $250,000 has Box 1 of $250,000 but Box 3 of $184,500. Box 5 (Medicare wages) has no cap, so it matches Box 1 for most employees.

Q: Do W-2 totals have to match my Form 941s?
A: Yes. The totals on Form W-3 — Box 1 wages, Box 2 federal withholding, Box 3 and Box 5 wages, and Boxes 4 and 6 taxes — must match the sum of the same figures on your four quarterly Forms 941. The IRS and SSA compare them, and a mismatch is one of the most common reasons employers receive a notice.
