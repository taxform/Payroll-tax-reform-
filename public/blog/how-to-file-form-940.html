How to File Form 940 (2026): FUTA Guide for Employers

Form 940, the Employer's Annual Federal Unemployment (FUTA) Tax Return, is the once-a-year return where employers report and pay the federal unemployment tax: 6.0% on the first $7,000 of each employee's wages.

The $42 questionLine-by-line guide2026 due datesDeposit rule
Quick answer: File once a year. Form 940 for calendar year 2026 is due Monday, February 1, 2027, because January 31, 2027 is a Sunday. If you deposited all of your FUTA tax on time and in full during the year, you have until February 10, 2027, to file. Your deposit may be due earlier: if your undeposited FUTA liability was more than $500 at the end of a quarter, deposit it by the last day of the next month.

This guide was compiled from IRS sources on November 11, 2026 and verifies line by line against the form and its instructions. As of this guide's publication on November 11, 2026, the current published Instructions for Form 940 on irs.gov are the 2025 revision. The 2026 revision follows the same structure — apply it to the 2026 tax year and check irs.gov for the final 2026 instructions before you file.

What Form 940 is — and what it is not

Form 940, the Employer's Annual Federal Unemployment (FUTA) Tax Return, is where you report the federal unemployment tax you owe for the year and work out the credit you get for state unemployment tax you paid.

FUTA, the Federal Unemployment Tax Act, funds the federal-state unemployment insurance system. Employers pay FUTA to the federal government; the states run their own unemployment programs and collect their own state unemployment tax (SUTA), on their own schedules and wage bases.

It is annual. One return per calendar year, filed in January or February for the year just ended. The tax itself, however, may have to be deposited quarterly during the year — the deposit rules are in Depositing FUTA tax.

It is employer-only. FUTA is paid entirely by the employer from its own funds. It is not withheld from employees' wages, it does not appear on their W-2s, and there is no employee share. If you are used to splitting Social Security and Medicare taxes with employees, set that habit aside here.

It is capped low. Only the first $7,000 of wages per employee per year is subject to FUTA. An employee who earns $7,000 in February stops accumulating FUTA wages for the rest of the year.

It is not your quarterly return. Form 940 does not report withheld income tax or Social Security and Medicare taxes — that is Form 941, filed quarterly. It does not report backup withholding either. It does not report state unemployment tax (SUTA) — you report that on your state's own unemployment return. And meeting the state deadline doesn't guarantee the federal credit: you must have paid the state tax on time and in full to claim the full credit (see Credit-reduction states).

Who must file Form 940

Under the general test, you are subject to FUTA tax and must file Form 940 for 2026 if you meet either of these conditions, in 2026 or in 2025:

  • The $1,500 wage test: you paid wages of $1,500 or more to employees in any calendar quarter; or
  • The 20-week test: you had one or more employees for at least some part of a day in any 20 or more different weeks. Count all full-time, part-time, and temporary employees. If your business is a successor to another business, you may also have to count the employees of the predecessor.

Meeting either test in either year makes you liable for the year, even if you later drop below the threshold. If your business was sold or transferred during the year, each employer who meets one of the tests must file — do not include wages paid by a predecessor employer, and do not combine payrolls to meet the test.

Special cases:

  • Household employers. A separate test applies: you paid cash wages of $1,000 or more (for all domestic employees combined) in any calendar quarter in 2025 or in 2026. Household FUTA is reported on Schedule H (Form 1040) instead of Form 940.
  • Agricultural employers. You file Form 940 if you paid cash wages of $20,000 or more to farmworkers during any calendar quarter in 2025 or 2026, or employed 10 or more farmworkers during some part of a day in any 20 or more different weeks in 2025 or 2026.
  • Generally exempt — no Form 940 required. Religious, educational, scientific, and charitable organizations described in section 501(c)(3); state and local governments and their instrumentalities; and, in many cases, organizations with employees who are exempt by statute. If you are unsure, check Topic No. 759 on irs.gov or ask a payroll professional — filing a return you don't owe is itself a chore.
  • No payments to employees in 2026. If you are not liable for FUTA tax for 2026 because you made no payments to employees, check box c ("No payments to employees in 2026") at the top of Form 940.
  • Closing the business. If this is a final return because you went out of business or stopped paying wages and will not be liable for filing Form 940 in the future, check box d ("Final: Business closed or stopped paying wages") at the top of Form 940. File for the period you were in business, including all wages paid up to the closing date.

How much is FUTA tax? The $42 question

The gross rate is 6.0% and the wage base is $7,000 per employee per year. That would be $420.00 per employee. But the rate most employers actually pay is 0.6% — $42.00 per employee per year — because the federal government gives employers a credit of up to 5.4% for state unemployment tax they have paid.

WhatRateOn the first $7,000 per employee
Gross FUTA6.0%$420.00
Less the maximum credit for state unemployment tax paidup to 5.4%−$378.00
Net FUTA, with the full credit0.6%$42.00

Why only $42 — and when you pay more. The 5.4% credit is a federal credit, calculated state by state, and it is not automatic. It is 5.4% only if you paid state unemployment tax (SUTA) on time and in full, in a state that is not a credit-reduction state, and only on wages that count for both FUTA and SUTA. If you paid state tax late, or your state was a credit-reduction state, the credit is reduced or denied — and that reduction is what turns a $42 bill into an employer-level problem.

Credit-reduction states: how the $42 becomes more

In a credit-reduction state, employers cannot claim the full 5.4% credit. The credit is reduced by 0.3% for each year that a state has had an unpaid federal loan balance for its unemployment program. If the reduction is 0.3%, the net rate is 0.9% and the tax is $63 per employee. If the reduction is 0.6%, it is $84 per employee, and so on — until at 5.4% reduction the full $420 is due.

No state is on the credit-reduction list for 2026 according to the list available on November 11, 2026. The Department of Labor publishes the credit-reduction list each year, and the final 2026 list had not been published when this guide was written. Before you file for 2026, check the Department of Labor's credit-reduction list and, if your state is listed, complete Schedule A (Form 940) for that state.

If your state was a credit-reduction state in a prior year, you will see the effect in your records: a state on the list for a year means employers in that state paid more FUTA that year. Schedule A, Form 940, is where you compute that extra tax.

Read the lines in order. Work from Part 2 before you get to Part 4 — the state credit calculation is what makes the difference between $420 per employee and $42.

Worked example: 8 employees, $25,000 each

Setup: Wilson's Hardware has eight full-time employees, each paid $25,000 in 2026 — total payroll $200,000. The business operates in only one state, which is not a credit-reduction state. State unemployment taxes were paid on time and in full, so the full 5.4% credit applies.

LineCalculation
1. Total payments to all employees8 × $25,000 = $200,000.00
2. Payments exempt from FUTA tax$0.00
3. Total payments after exempt payments$200,000.00
4. Total FUTA taxable wages8 × $7,000 = $56,000.00
5. FUTA tax before credits$56,000 × 6.0% = $3,360.00
6. State credit(8 × $7,000) × 5.4% = $3,024.00 credit
7. Total FUTA tax due$3,360.00 − $3,024.00 = $336.00
8. Tax deposited during the year$0.00
9. Balance due with the return$336.00

That is $42.00 per employee — 8 × $42.00 = $336.00. Why no deposit was due: the quarterly liability was $300.00 in Q1 (8 × $6,250 × 0.006), $36.00 in Q2 (each employee's remaining $750 in wages × 8 × 0.6%), and $0 in Q3 and Q4, because the $7,000 wage base was exhausted by the second quarter. That is a total undeposited liability of $336 at year-end, and it never exceeded $500, so no deposit was due during the year. The $336 is paid with the return.

Contrast: a larger employer has a different story. With 30 employees who were each paid $10,000 in Q1 alone, the FUTA taxable wages of those employees in Q1 are 30 × $7,000 = $210,000, for a liability of $210,000 × 0.006 = $1,260 — well over $500 — and Q1's deposit would be due April 30, 2026.

When is Form 940 due? 2026 filing and deposit deadlines

Form 940 is due Monday, February 1, 2027 for the 2026 tax year. If the due date falls on a Saturday or Sunday, or on a legal holiday, the return is due on the next business day.

Form 940 tax yearFiling deadline
2026 tax yearMonday, February 1, 2027 (January 31, 2027 is a Sunday; February 10, 2027 if you deposited all of your FUTA tax on time and in full)

Do not file early unless the payroll work is finished. Do not file on time when the work is incorrect — late filing costs more than getting it right.

Depositing FUTA tax

FUTA tax deposits work like income tax deposits: they have a quarterly trigger and a monthly deadline.

  • Deposit rule: At the end of each quarter — March 31, June 30, September 30, and December 31 — take the undeposited FUTA tax liability, add it to the undeposited liability from previous quarters, and deposit the total if it is more than $500.
  • Deposit due dates: Deposit by the last day of the month after the quarter ends. The deposit deadlines for 2026 tax are: Q1 — Thursday, April 30, 2026; Q2 — Friday, July 31, 2026; Q3 — Monday, November 2, 2026; Q4 — Monday, February 1, 2027.
  • Deposit only by EFT: You must deposit by electronic funds transfer. Use EFTPS. Do not send a deposit with Form 940 or with any other form.
  • If your undeposited liability is $500 or less at quarter-end, carry it over to the next quarter. If it is $500 or less at the end of the fourth quarter, pay it with your Form 940 by February 1, 2027.
  • Depositing after a credit-reduction state is listed: If you owe additional tax because your state is on the credit-reduction list, deposit it as soon as the additional liability is known.

How to file Form 940

Here is the short list:

  • Get the form. Download Form 940 and its instructions from irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-940 (also called the Employer's Annual Federal Unemployment (FUTA) Tax Return). If you use payroll software, it can prepare Form 940 for you.
  • Compute the total FUTA taxable wages. Total payments to all employees, minus payments exempt from FUTA tax, minus wages above $7,000 per employee, gives you the FUTA taxable wages. Check line by line — the risk is counting the full annual payroll instead of only the first $7,000 per employee.
  • Apply the state credit. Form 940 is where you claim the state credit — both the 5.4% credit and any credit-reduction adjustment. If you paid state unemployment taxes on time and in full, you get the full credit.
  • Pay what is due. FUTA tax is due when the return is due. If your balance due is more than $500, deposit the tax before you file — the return is not the way to send the deposit. Deposits are due by EFTPS; the payment with Form 940 is the balance due after you deduct the deposits you made.
  • Keep your records. Keep your payroll records for at least four years from the later of the due date of Form 940 or the date you paid the tax.

Penalties: filing late, depositing late, and being wrong

Failure to file. If you file late, and you owe tax, the penalty is 5% per month of your unpaid balance, up to 25% of what you owe. If you file more than 60 days after the due date, the minimum is the lesser of $485 or 100% of the tax owed (for returns due after December 31, 2025).

Failure to pay. If you pay late, but your return was timely, the penalty is 0.5% per month of your unpaid balance, up to 25% of the tax.

Failure to deposit. If you deposit late, the penalty is a percentage of the deposit, depending on how late it was:

How late the deposit wasPenalty
1–5 days late2% of the deposit
6–15 days late5% of the deposit
More than 15 days late10% of the deposit
Still unpaid more than 10 days after the first bill from the IRS15% of the deposit

Failure to deposit on time — warning. The failure-to-deposit penalty is assessed per deposit. A small mistake in Q1 can cost you a large share of that quarter's tax, and continuing to miss deposits compounds the penalty across quarters. If you think there is a good reason for your failure, the IRS says penalties may be abated for reasonable cause — but a lack of funds is not generally reasonable cause.

Correcting a mistake: Form 940-X

If you discover an error on a Form 940 you have already filed, correct it with Form 940-X — the Adjusted Employer's Annual Federal Unemployment (FUTA) Tax Return or Claim for Refund. File Form 940-X to correct a previously filed Form 940. If you owe tax, pay it with Form 940-X. You can also use Form 940-X to claim a credit or a refund if you overpaid.

Five common Form 940 mistakes

  1. Using the full payroll as the wage base. Only the first $7,000 of wages per employee, per year, is subject to FUTA. Don't multiply your total payroll by 0.6%.
  2. Missing the $500 deposit trigger. The test is the undeposited liability at the end of a quarter. If it is more than $500, deposit by the last day of the next month.
  3. Assuming the state credit is automatic. Late or unpaid state tax reduces the credit. In a credit-reduction state, the credit is reduced by 0.3% per year of outstanding loan.
  4. Filing Form 940 for the wrong year. File the return for the year you paid the wages, not for the year you happen to be filing in.
  5. Treating FUTA as an employee tax. FUTA is a federal tax on the employer only. Do not withhold FUTA from employees' wages, and do not count it on their W-2s.

Frequently asked questions

When is Form 940 due for 2026?

Form 940 for calendar year 2026 is due Monday, February 1, 2027, because January 31, 2027 is a Sunday. If you deposited all of your FUTA tax on time and in full during the year, you have until February 10, 2027 to file.

How much is FUTA tax per employee?

FUTA is 6.0% on the first $7,000 of each employee's wages, or $420.00 per employee. With the full 5.4% state credit, the net rate is 0.6%, or $42.00 per employee per year.

Who must file Form 940?

You must file Form 940 if you paid wages of $1,500 or more to employees in any calendar quarter in 2026 or 2025, or you had one or more employees for at least some part of a day in any 20 or more different weeks in 2026 or 2025. Household and agricultural employers have different tests.

When do I have to deposit FUTA tax?

Deposit FUTA tax quarterly if your undeposited liability is more than $500 at the end of a quarter. The deposit is due the last day of the month after the quarter ends: April 30, July 31, November 2, 2026, and February 1, 2027 for the 2026 quarters.

Does Form 940 affect what I pay in state unemployment tax?

No. Form 940 reports federal unemployment tax only. You report and pay state unemployment tax (SUTA) on your state return, and you cannot claim the full federal credit for state tax unless you paid it on time and in full.

What is the difference between Form 940 and Form 941?

Form 940 is the annual return for federal unemployment (FUTA) tax, paid entirely by the employer. Form 941 is the quarterly return for withheld income tax and Social Security and Medicare taxes.

What is a credit-reduction state?

A credit-reduction state is a state that has an outstanding federal loan balance for its unemployment program. Employers in such a state cannot claim the full 5.4% FUTA credit, so they pay more than $42 per employee. No state is on the credit-reduction list for 2026 according to the list available on November 11, 2026, and the final list was not yet published.

What if I made a mistake on Form 940?

Correct a mistake with Form 940-X, Adjusted Employer's Annual Federal Unemployment (FUTA) Tax Return or Claim for Refund. File it to correct a previously filed Form 940, and pay any additional tax with it.
