California Payroll Taxes 2026: Employer Guide (Withholding, SUI, SDI)

California is the most complex state in the country for payroll: four separate state payroll taxes, its own withholding form, its own quarterly returns, and a minimum wage that rises every year. This guide covers all four taxes for 2026 — the rates, the wage bases, how to register, when to file and deposit, and what a $60,000 employee actually costs a California employer, on top of the federal taxes.

Quick answer: California employers handle four state payroll taxes in 2026, all administered by the Employment Development Department (EDD): Unemployment Insurance (UI) at 1.5%–6.2% on the first $7,000 of each employee's wages (3.4% for new employers), Employment Training Tax (ETT) at 0.1% on the first $7,000, State Disability Insurance (SDI) withheld from employees at 1.3% of all wages with no wage cap, and Personal Income Tax (PIT) withheld per the employee's Form DE 4. Quarterly returns (DE 9 and DE 9C) are due April 1, July 1, October 1, and January 1.

Figures verified November 12, 2026 against the California EDD's 2026 Household Employer's Guide (DE 8829) and the EDD 2026 Payroll Tax Calendar, and the Department of Industrial Relations for the minimum wage. The 2026 FUTA credit-reduction list was not final on that date — see the note in the federal section below. If you are reading this after the IRS publishes the 2026 Schedule A (Form 940), check the current list before filing.

Two of the four come out of the employer's pocket (UI and ETT); two are withheld from the employee's paycheck and remitted by the employer (SDI and PIT). The employer is responsible for all four — the EDD holds you liable even for the two that come out of employees' wages.

Unemployment Insurance (UI / SUI): the employer's rate

UI is employer-only. The EDD sets your rate by experience rating: it tracks your UI reserve account — the benefits charged against former employees versus the contributions you have paid. New employers start at a flat 3.4% for two to three years before experience rating begins.

  • 2026 range: 1.5% to 6.2%, under Schedule F+ — Schedule F plus a 15% emergency surcharge, unchanged from 2025.
  • Wage base: only the first $7,000 paid to each employee per calendar year. Once an employee passes $7,000, UI stops for that employee for the rest of the year.
  • Cost per employee: $238 a year at the 3.4% new-employer rate; $434 at the top 6.2% rate.
  • Your rate: the EDD mails a Notice of Contribution Rates and Statement of UI Reserve Account (Form DE 2088) each December with your rate for the coming year. It is also visible in e-Services for Business.
  • Benefit side: the 2026 UI maximum weekly benefit amount is $450.

Because the wage base is only $7,000, California UI is a small cost for most employers — the expensive part of California payroll is the withholding side, not UI. Compare that with the federal FUTA tax: 6.0% on the same first $7,000, usually 0.6% ($42) after the state credit. See our Form 940 guide.

Employment Training Tax (ETT): 0.1% on the first $7,000

ETT is a flat 0.1% employer tax on the first $7,000 of each employee's wages — $7 per employee per year at most. It funds job training programs. It is reported and paid on the same returns as UI (DE 9), so there is no separate filing — but do not forget it exists: it is a line on the return, and a return that omits it will not reconcile.

State Disability Insurance (SDI): 1.3% of all wages, no cap

SDI is withheld entirely from the employee's wages — the employer contributes nothing directly — but the employer withholds it, reports it, and remits it. The 2026 rate is 1.3%, up from 1.2% in 2025.

  • No wage cap. Senate Bill 951 removed the SDI taxable wage limit effective January 1, 2024. Before that, only wages up to $153,164 (2023) were subject. Since 2024, every dollar of wages is subject to the 1.3% withholding.
  • What it funds: SDI covers both State Disability Insurance and Paid Family Leave (PFL) benefits. An employee earning $60,000 has $780 a year withheld for SDI.
  • High earners notice it. Because there is no cap, a $200,000 employee has $2,600 withheld — the most common surprise for employers moving staff to California from a state with a capped disability tax.

Personal Income Tax (PIT) withholding: the DE 4

California employers withhold state income tax from employee wages using Form DE 4 (Employee's Withholding Allowance Certificate), the state equivalent of the federal W-4, and the Franchise Tax Board's withholding tables. Marginal rates run from 1% to 13.3%, so the amount varies sharply by employee — there is no single PIT rate to quote, and no flat-rate shortcut that is safe for every employee.

The $350 rule — the deposit trigger that catches new employers. PIT and SDI withholdings are deposited with Form DE 88. How often you must deposit depends on how much PIT you withhold: if you withhold more than $350 in PIT in a pay period, the EDD may require deposits more often than quarterly — monthly, semiweekly, or next-day for large amounts. Check your PIT withholding every payroll; a growing payroll can quietly move you from quarterly to monthly deposits, and the deposit schedule is the most common source of EDD penalties for small employers. UI and ETT, by contrast, are due quarterly with the return.

Registering as a California employer

Register with the EDD within 15 days of paying $100 or more in wages in a calendar quarter, through e-Services for Business. You receive an employer payroll tax account number, which goes on every filing. Registration is online; paper registration is the slow route.

  • New hires: report every newly hired or rehired employee to the New Employee Registry within 20 calendar days of their start-of-work date.
  • Electronic filing and payment are mandatory. All employers must electronically submit employment tax returns, wage reports, and payroll tax deposits to the EDD — the e-file and e-pay mandate applies to every employer, not just large ones.
  • Before your first payroll: get your federal EIN, register with the EDD, collect a DE 4 and a W-4 from each employee, and confirm your UI rate (3.4% if you are new). Our first-payroll checklist walks through the federal side.

Filing and deposit deadlines for 2026

Two quarterly returns carry the state side: DE 9 (Quarterly Contribution Return and Report of Wages — totals for the quarter) and DE 9C (the wage detail, employee by employee, with PIT withholdings). Both are due the same day.

Deposits (Form DE 88) follow your deposit schedule — quarterly for small PIT amounts, monthly or more often once PIT withholding passes $350 in a pay period. If a due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the next business day is the deadline. Note the Q3 delinquent date is November 2, not October 31, because October 31, 2026 is a Saturday.

These are the state dates. The federal dates run alongside them: Form 941 quarterly, Form 940 annually (due February 1, 2027), and W-2s to employees by February 1, 2027. The federal layer: 941, 940, W-2 — and the credit-reduction question

California payroll is federal payroll plus the four state taxes. You still file Form 941 quarterly, Form 940 annually, and W-2s by February 1, 2027 — see our Form 941 guide and Form 940 guide.

Credit-reduction warning: California has appeared on the federal FUTA credit-reduction list in recent years, which raises the effective FUTA rate above 0.6% for employers who paid wages there. The 2026 list was not final when this guide was drafted on November 12, 2026 — the Department of Labor makes its determination each November 10, and the IRS publishes the final list on Schedule A (Form 940). The worked example above assumes the full credit. Before you file Form 940, check the current Schedule A and re-figure the FUTA line if California is listed.

Minimum wage and wage-and-hour rules that change the payroll

California's statewide minimum wage is $16.90 per hour in 2026 for all employers, set by the Department of Industrial Relations — up from $16.50 in 2025. Higher rates apply in some industries (fast-food workers $20 per hour since April 2024) and in cities with local ordinances, so check the local rate where each employee works. The minimum wage also sets the salary threshold for exempt employees (twice the minimum wage for full-time work), so a minimum-wage rise can reclassify salaried staff — check exemption status each January, not just pay rates.

5 mistakes that trigger EDD notices

  1. Forgetting SDI has no cap. Employers who moved from a state with a capped disability tax — or who remember the pre-2024 $153,164 cap — under-withhold SDI on high earners. Since January 1, 2024, 1.3% applies to every dollar.
  2. Missing the $350 PIT deposit trigger. A growing payroll can move you from quarterly to monthly deposits without any notice from the EDD. Check PIT withheld every pay period; late deposits draw penalties even when the quarterly return is on time.
  3. Registering late. The 15-day registration window runs from the first $100 of wages in a quarter, not from your first hire's start date. Register before the first payroll, not after it.
  4. Using the federal W-4 for state withholding. California PIT withholding follows the DE 4 and the FTB tables, not the W-4. An employee who completes only a W-4 has no valid state withholding certificate on file.
  5. Applying UI to all wages. UI and ETT stop at $7,000 per employee per year. Taxing a $60,000 salary at 3.4% overstates UI by $1,802 per employee — and the DE 9 will not reconcile.

Frequently asked questions

Q: What are California payroll taxes for employers in 2026?
A: Four state taxes, all run by the EDD: UI at 1.5%–6.2% on the first $7,000 of each employee's wages, ETT at 0.1% on the first $7,000, SDI withheld from employees at 1.3% of all wages with no cap, and PIT withheld per the employee's DE 4. UI and ETT are employer-paid; SDI and PIT are withheld from pay.

Q: What is the California SUI rate for a new employer in 2026?
A: A flat 3.4% for two to three years, on the first $7,000 of each employee's wages — $238 per employee per year. After that the EDD assigns an experience rate under Schedule F+, from 1.5% to 6.2%. Your rate is on your annual DE 2088 notice, mailed each December.

Q: What is the California SDI rate for 2026?
A: 1.3% of all subject wages, withheld from the employee's paycheck, with no wage cap. Senate Bill 951 removed the cap effective January 1, 2024. The rate rose from 1.2% in 2025 to 1.3% in 2026, and it funds both State Disability Insurance and Paid Family Leave.

Q: When are California DE 9 and DE 9C due in 2026?
A: Due April 1, July 1, October 1, and January 1 for each quarter, and delinquent if not filed by April 30, July 31, November 2, and February 1, 2027. The fourth-quarter 2026 returns are due January 1, 2027 and delinquent February 1, 2027.

Q: How do I register as a California employer?
A: Register with the EDD within 15 days of paying $100 or more in wages in a calendar quarter, through e-Services for Business. You receive an employer payroll tax account number for all filings. Report each new hire to the New Employee Registry within 20 days of their start date.

Q: Does California have state income tax withholding?
A: Yes. Employers withhold PIT based on the employee's Form DE 4 and the Franchise Tax Board withholding tables, at marginal rates from 1% to 13.3%. If you withhold more than $350 in PIT in a pay period, the EDD may require deposits more often than quarterly.

Q: What is California's minimum wage in 2026?
A: $16.90 per hour statewide for all employers, set by the Department of Industrial Relations — up from $16.50 in 2025. Fast-food workers earn at least $20 per hour, and many cities set higher local minimums, so check the local rate where your employees work.

Q: Is California a FUTA credit-reduction state for 2026?
A: The 2026 list was not final when this guide was drafted on November 12, 2026. The Department of Labor determines credit-reduction states each November 10, and the IRS publishes the final list on Schedule A (Form 940). Check the current Schedule A before you file — do not rely on last year's list.
