Best Payroll Software for a 1-Employee Business (2026)

Payroll software guide · Published November 16, 2026

Hiring your first employee changes the paperwork more than the payroll. One person on staff means withholding, tax deposits, quarterly filings, a W-2, new-hire reporting, and a state unemployment account — and the penalties for getting any of it wrong do not scale down because you only have one employee. Payroll software takes most of that off your plate for $21 to $56 a month. This guide prices the realistic options for exactly one employee, shows the first-year cost of each, and says plainly which one to pick and which ones to skip.

Quick answer: The cheapest full-service payroll for one employee is Deel US Payroll at $29 a month, with no base fee. The cheapest from a traditional small-business payroll vendor is SurePayroll Full Service at $36 a month, followed by Patriot Full Service at $42. The best value for most one-employee employers is OnPay at $55 a month — the same price as Gusto Simple, but multi-state payroll is included and the first month is free. If you already pay for QuickBooks Online, add Core payroll at $56.50 a month; if you do not, do not buy QuickBooks just for payroll, because the subscription underneath costs another $38 a month. Skip Deel EOR ($599 a month per employee) and Rippling's full suite — both are built for companies far bigger than yours.

What a 1-employee employer actually needs

One employee creates the same compliance obligations as ten, just with smaller numbers. Here is what has to happen every pay period, every quarter, and every year — and what the software should be doing for you:

  • Calculate gross-to-net pay. Withhold federal income tax, the employee's Social Security (6.2% of wages up to the $184,500 wage base in 2026) and Medicare (1.45%), and any state and local withholding. Add the employer's matching Social Security and Medicare on top.
  • Pay the employee. Direct deposit, on schedule. Most providers include two-day direct deposit; same-day or next-day deposit is usually an add-on or a higher tier.
  • Deposit the taxes on time. Federal deposits go through EFTPS on a monthly schedule for a new employer, due by the 15th of the following month. State deposits follow your state's schedule. A missed federal deposit costs 2% to 10% of the late amount in penalties.
  • File the returns. Form 941 every quarter, Form 940 once a year for FUTA, plus state withholding and unemployment (SUTA) returns. A full-service plan files all of these for you; a basic plan does not.
  • Report the new hire. Every state requires new employees to be reported to a state directory, usually within 10 to 20 days of hire. Full-service providers handle this.
  • Issue the W-2. Due to the employee (and the Social Security Administration) by February 1, 2027 for 2026 wages. Most providers include it; SurePayroll charges for year-end forms (see below).
  • Register for state tax accounts. Before your first payroll, you need a state withholding account and a SUTA (state unemployment) account. The software does not do this for you — see the section below.

What to skip

At one employee, do not pay for: an HR suite (handbooks, performance reviews, applicant tracking), benefits administration (until you actually offer health insurance or a 401(k)), time tracking (if your employee is salaried), a PEO (a co-employment arrangement that makes sense at 10-plus employees, not one), or an employer-of-record service (for hiring in countries where you have no entity — not your situation). Every dollar spent on those features is a dollar that buys nothing for a one-person payroll.

The picks

Cheapest full-service: Deel US Payroll, $29 a month. No base fee, so one employee costs $29 flat. It calculates pay, files federal, state, and local taxes, and runs direct deposit. The caveat is that Deel is built for global hiring and its US payroll product is self-service — support and hand-holding are lighter than Gusto or OnPay, and you should confirm it covers your state before signing up. It is the right pick if you are comfortable setting things up yourself and want the lowest bill.

Cheapest from a traditional payroll vendor: SurePayroll Full Service, $36 a month. SurePayroll is owned by Paychex and has been selling small-business payroll for years. Full Service files federal, state, and local taxes, includes unlimited payroll runs and two-day direct deposit, and charges a flat $9.99 a month for multi-state payroll. Watch the extras: year-end W-2 forms cost $50 plus $5 per form, and local tax filing carries a separate fee where it applies. First year with the W-2: $487.

Best value: OnPay, $55 a month. One plan, everything in it: unlimited payroll runs, automated tax filings in all 50 states, W-2s and 1099s included, garnishments, and the same $6 rate whether the person is a W-2 employee or a 1099 contractor. The first month is free, so the first year is $605. There are no tiers to outgrow and no state surcharge — the two things that quietly raise Gusto's price.

Best if you already use QuickBooks Online: QuickBooks Payroll Core, $56.50 a month. Full-service payroll inside the accounting software you already pay for: tax calculation, filing, and payment, next-day direct deposit, and 1099 e-filing. Intuit usually offers 50% off the base fee for the first three months. Do not buy QuickBooks Online just to get the payroll — at $94.50 a month combined, it is the priciest route here.

Cheapest if you file taxes yourself: Patriot Basic, $21 a month. You get the pay calculation, direct deposit, and unlimited runs, and you handle deposits and filings yourself through EFTPS and your state. It only saves money if you actually do the filings on time — one late deposit wipes out the savings. If you are not sure you will file on time every quarter, pay the extra $21 a month for Patriot Full Service and let them file.

What the software does not do for you

Payroll software files and pays — it does not set your business up as an employer. Before your first payroll, you still have to:

  • Register for state tax accounts. A state withholding account and a SUTA (state unemployment insurance) account. The software files the returns; it does not register you. Most states let you register online in a day or two, but some take weeks — start before your first payday, not after it.
  • Set up workers' compensation insurance. Almost every state requires it once you have an employee, and it is a separate insurance purchase. Gusto and others offer pay-as-you-go workers' comp administration, but the policy itself is yours to buy.
  • Classify the worker correctly. Employee or independent contractor is a legal determination, not a software setting. Misclassifying an employee as a contractor is one of the most expensive mistakes a small employer can make.
  • Decide exempt vs. non-exempt status. Whether your employee is owed overtime under the Fair Labor Standards Act depends on their duties and pay, not on what the software defaults to.
  • Handle local registrations. Some cities and counties have their own payroll taxes or employer registrations (New York City, Philadelphia, San Francisco, and others). SurePayroll charges a separate fee for local tax filing where it applies; other providers vary.

None of this is a reason to skip the software — it is a reason to budget one afternoon for setup before the first payday, and to check the setup with your state's employer registration page or your accountant.

5 mistakes that cost 1-employee employers money

  1. Buying a plan built for 50 people. A PEO or an employer-of-record service charges $79 to $599 per employee a month for HR, benefits, and compliance infrastructure. At one employee, you are paying for an HR department you do not have. Deel EOR at $599 a month is twenty times the cost of Deel US Payroll at $29 — for the same one employee.
  2. Picking a cheap plan that does not file your taxes. Patriot Basic at $21 and SurePayroll No Tax Filing at $24 look like bargains until a deposit is missed. A federal deposit that is 6 to 15 days late costs 5% of the late amount; more than 15 days costs 10%. On a $3,000 monthly deposit, that is $150 to $300 — more than the annual savings from the cheaper plan.
  3. Comparing per-employee prices without the base fee. At one employee, the base fee is 60% to 90% of the bill. Deel's $29 per employee with no base fee beats SurePayroll's $7 per employee precisely because SurePayroll also charges a $29 base fee. Always multiply out the total for your headcount.
  4. Buying QuickBooks Payroll without already using QuickBooks Online. The payroll add-on is reasonably priced at $56.50 a month. The $38-a-month accounting subscription underneath it is not payroll at all — and if you do not need QuickBooks Online for your books, you are paying $456 a year for software you did not want.
  5. Forgetting the year-end and multi-state fees. SurePayroll's $55 year-end form fee, Gusto's Plus upgrade if your employee crosses state lines ($37 a month more), and Patriot's $12-a-month per-state fee all sit outside the headline price. Ask each provider what the first year costs in total, including the W-2, before you sign up.

Frequently asked questions

Q: What is the cheapest payroll software for 1 employee?

A: Deel US Payroll is the cheapest full-service option at $29 a month for one employee, with no base fee. Among traditional small-business payroll vendors, SurePayroll Full Service is cheapest at $36 a month ($29 base plus $7 per employee), followed by Patriot Full Service at $42 a month ($37 plus $5). The cheapest option of any kind is Patriot Basic at $21 a month, but it does not file your payroll taxes — you do that yourself.

Q: Do I need payroll software for just one employee?

A: You do not legally need it — you can calculate withholding, pay by direct deposit, and file Form 941 and Form 940 yourself. But for one employee, payroll software costs $21 to $56 a month and takes over the parts most likely to go wrong: calculating withholding, depositing taxes on time, filing quarterly and annual returns, and issuing the W-2. The cost of one missed deposit (a 2% to 10% failure-to-deposit penalty) is usually more than several months of software.

Q: Can I do payroll myself for 1 employee without software?

A: Yes. You calculate gross-to-net pay, withhold federal income tax and the employee's share of Social Security (6.2% up to the $184,500 wage base in 2026) and Medicare (1.45%), deposit the taxes through EFTPS on your monthly schedule, file Form 941 quarterly and Form 940 annually, report the new hire to your state, and issue a W-2 by February 1, 2027. It is doable for one employee, and our EFTPS, Form 941, and Form 940 guides walk through each step — but you own every deadline yourself, and there is no reminder system.

Q: Does QuickBooks Payroll require a QuickBooks Online subscription?

A: Yes. QuickBooks Payroll (Core, Premium, or Elite) is an add-on to QuickBooks Online, not a standalone product. Core costs $50 a month plus $6.50 per employee, but that is on top of a QuickBooks Online subscription — Simple Start is $38 a month at list price. So a 1-employee business not already on QuickBooks Online pays $94.50 a month in total for Core plus Simple Start. If you already pay for QuickBooks Online, the payroll add-on alone is $56.50 a month for one employee.

Q: What does payroll software actually file for me?

A: A full-service plan files and pays your payroll taxes for you: federal deposits through EFTPS, Form 941 quarterly, Form 940 annually, and your state withholding and unemployment returns. It also issues the employee's W-2 at year end and usually handles new-hire reporting to the state. A basic or self-service plan does the pay calculation and direct deposit but leaves the tax deposits and filings to you — check which kind a plan is before you buy, because the price gap between the two is the tax-filing work.

Q: How much does payroll software cost per year for 1 employee?

A: For one employee on a full-service plan that files your taxes: Deel US Payroll $348 a year, SurePayroll Full Service $432 a year (plus $55 for year-end W-2 forms), Patriot Full Service $504 a year, Gusto Simple and OnPay $660 a year, QuickBooks Core $678 a year for payroll alone or $1,134 a year with a QuickBooks Online Simple Start subscription, and Rippling roughly $672 a year at list prices (quote required). If you file taxes yourself, Patriot Basic is $252 a year.

Q: Is Gusto or OnPay better for a 1-employee business?

A: Both cost $55 a month for one employee ($49 base plus $6 per person) and both are full-service, so the difference is in the details. OnPay includes multi-state payroll at no extra cost and charges the same $6 rate for 1099 contractors, and the first month is free. Gusto Simple covers a single state only — if your employee works in more than one state you need Gusto Plus at $92 a month for one employee — but Gusto has a more polished interface, more integrations, and built-in benefits administration. For a one-employee business that may ever cross state lines, OnPay is the better value; for a single-state employer who wants the nicest interface, Gusto is.

Q: What should I avoid when choosing payroll software for 1 employee?

A: Avoid three things. First, platforms built for larger companies — PEO and employer-of-record services such as Deel EOR at $599 a month per employee and Rippling's full HR/IT/finance suite — which charge enterprise prices for features a one-employee business will never use. Second, a cheap plan that does not file your taxes, unless you genuinely plan to do the deposits and filings yourself; a missed deposit costs 2% to 10% in penalties. Third, comparing per-employee prices without the base fee: every provider here charges a base fee plus a per-employee fee, and the base fee is most of the bill at one employee.
