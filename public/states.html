California

Withholding Agency: Employment Development Department (EDD)

California features graduated personal income tax rates up to 13.3%, mandatory State Disability Insurance (SDI) with uncapped wages, and an SUI wage base of $7,000.

Withholding Agency: NY Department of Taxation and Finance (DTF)

Texas

Agency: Texas Workforce Commission (TWC)

Texas has no individual state income tax. Employers are not required to withhold state income tax, but must register with TWC for State Unemployment Insurance (SUI wage base $9,000).

Florida

Agency: Florida Department of Revenue (FDOR)

Florida has no personal income tax. Employers do not withhold state income tax, but must remit Florida Reemployment Tax (SUI) on the first $7,000 of wages per employee.

Multi-State Remote Work Payroll Considerations

The Physical Work Location Principle

Under general tax nexus rules, employers must register, withhold state income tax, and pay SUI in the state where the employee physically sits and performs work, even if the business is located in another state.

Reciprocal Tax Agreements

Several neighboring states (such as Pennsylvania and New Jersey, or Illinois and Iowa) have reciprocal agreements. These allow employees living in one state and working in another to pay income taxes only to their home state of residence.

Convenience of the Employer Rule

A small number of states (including New York, Pennsylvania, and Nebraska) enforce a "convenience rule", which taxes remote employees based on their company's assigned office unless the remote arrangement is an absolute business necessity.

Frequently Asked Questions About State Payroll Taxes

Which states have no individual state income tax?

Nine U.S. states have no individual earned income tax: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire (which taxes only dividend and interest income), South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, and Wyoming. Employers must still register for State Unemployment Insurance (SUI).

How does remote work affect state payroll taxes?

In general, employers must withhold state income tax and pay State Unemployment Insurance in the state where the employee physically performs the work, not necessarily where the company is headquartered.

What is State Unemployment Insurance (SUTA/SUI)?

SUI is an employer-paid state tax that funds unemployment benefits. Each state sets an annual taxable wage base (from $7,000 up to over $60,000) and assigns an experience rate to each employer based on workforce turnover.
