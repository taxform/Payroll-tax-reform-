Employer Payroll Tax by Salary (2026)

How much does an employer pay in federal payroll taxes on top of a salary? We calculated the 2026 bill for 25 salary levels, from $20,000 to $500,000, using the IRS and SSA rules for the year. All of the data is free to reuse with credit.

By PayrollTaxReform.com · Published November 10, 2026 · Methodology · Download the data

Key findings

$3,867

An employee paid $50,000 costs the employer $3,867 in federal payroll taxes in 2026, or 7.73% on top of salary, before state taxes and benefits.

7.86% → 3.75%

The employer's effective federal rate falls as pay rises. It is 7.86% on a $20,000 salary and 3.75% on a $500,000 salary, because Social Security stops at the $184,500 wage base.

+55.7%

The Social Security wage base has risen 55.7% since 2016, from $118,500 to $184,500. The most an employer can owe per employee rose $4,092, to $11,439.

What the numbers mean for employers

Budget about 7.7% for federal taxes on most salaries. For salaries from $20,000 to $184,500, the employer pays 7.65% in Social Security and Medicare plus $42 of FUTA. That puts the federal cost between 7.67% and 7.86% of pay.

State taxes come on top. State unemployment insurance, and in some states paid leave or training taxes, usually add between a few hundred and a few thousand dollars per employee. Rates vary by state and by each employer's claims history. See the state guides or run your numbers in the payroll tax calculator.

The rate drops above the wage base. Past $184,500, only the 1.45% Medicare tax continues. So a $500,000 salary costs the employer $18,731, less than 1.4 times the $14,156 bill on a $184,500 salary.

The wage base keeps rising. SSA raises the base each year in line with national average wages. Employers with well-paid staff should expect their maximum Social Security cost to climb again in 2027.

Methodology

  • Each row is one employee paid the listed salary in calendar year 2026.
  • Social Security: 6.2% employer share on wages up to $184,500 (SSA).
  • Medicare: 1.45% employer share on all wages. The 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax applies only to the employee (IRS Topic 560).
  • FUTA: 6.0% on the first $7,000 of wages, less the full 5.4% credit for timely state unemployment payments, which leaves $42 (IRS Publication 15). Employers in FUTA credit reduction states pay more.
  • Not included: state unemployment insurance, state and local payroll taxes, workers' compensation, and benefits.
  • Figures are rounded to the cent. The calculation is reproducible from the published rates, and the full results are in the download.
