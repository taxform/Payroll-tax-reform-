What Are Employer Payroll Taxes?

When a business hires employees, compensation involves two distinct categories of payroll taxes: taxes withheld from employee wages (trust fund taxes) and taxes paid directly by the employer.

Key Takeaway for Employers: Beyond an employee's agreed gross wages, an employer must budget approximately 7.65% to 11%+ in additional employer payroll taxes (FICA matching + FUTA + state unemployment insurance), plus any optional benefits, workers' compensation insurance, and local employer taxes.

Deep Dive: Federal Payroll Tax Components

FICA Component

1. Social Security Tax (OASDI)

The Federal Insurance Contributions Act (FICA) mandates a 6.2% tax on employee gross earnings, which the employer must match dollar-for-dollar with an additional 6.2%.

Once an employee's cumulative year-to-date wages cross the annual Social Security wage threshold, both withholdings and employer contributions stop for the remainder of that calendar year.

FICA Component

2. Medicare Tax & Additional Surcharge

Both employer and employee contribute 1.45% of all wages toward Medicare with no annual limit.

When an individual employee earns more than $200,000 in a calendar year, employers must withhold an extra 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax from the excess wages. Employers do not match this 0.9% surcharge.

FUTA Component

3. Federal Unemployment (FUTA)

FUTA provides funds for state unemployment agencies. The statutory rate is 6.0% on the first $7,000 of wages.

Employers who pay state unemployment taxes on time generally receive a credit of up to 5.4%, reducing their effective federal FUTA rate to just 0.6% ($42 maximum per eligible employee per year).

Key Payroll Tax Forms Every Employer Must File

Filing payroll taxes accurately and on time protects business owners from severe personal liability penalties. The primary IRS returns include:

IRS Form 941 (Quarterly)

Employer's Quarterly Federal Tax Return. Reports federal income taxes withheld, employee and employer Social Security tax, and Medicare tax. Filed four times a year.

Due Dates: April 30, July 31, October 31, January 31.

IRS Form 940 (Annual)

Employer's Annual Federal Unemployment (FUTA) Tax Return. Calculates annual FUTA tax liability, verifies state unemployment tax credits, and reconciles deposits.

Due Date: January 31 of each year.

Forms W-2 & W-3 (Annual)

Wage and Tax Statements. Reports annual employee earnings and tax withholdings to employees, the Social Security Administration (SSA), and state revenue departments.

Due Date: January 31 for both employee copies and SSA transmittal.

Common Payroll Compliance Traps & How to Avoid Penalties

1. The Trust Fund Recovery Penalty

Taxes withheld from employee paychecks (FIT and employee FICA) are legally considered trust funds held on behalf of the United States. Under IRC § 6672, officers and responsible individuals can be held personally liable for 100% of unpaid trust fund taxes, regardless of corporate bankruptcy.

2. Worker Misclassification (1099 vs W-2)

Treating employees as independent contractors to avoid employer payroll taxes is heavily audited by the IRS and state labor departments. Misclassification can trigger retroactive FICA assessments, back wages, workers' comp penalties, and interest charges.

3. Missing Deposit Schedules (EFTPS)

Employers must follow either monthly or semi-weekly deposit schedules based on their four-quarter lookback period. Deposits must be transmitted through the Electronic Federal Tax Payment System (EFTPS) on time to prevent Failure to Deposit penalties (up to 15%).

Frequently Asked Questions About Payroll Taxes

What taxes do employers pay vs employees?

Employers and employees split FICA taxes equally: 6.2% for Social Security and 1.45% for Medicare each. In addition, employers pay 100% of federal unemployment tax (FUTA) and state unemployment insurance (SUTA), while employees pay federal and state income tax withholding deducted from their wages.

What is the 2026 Social Security wage base limit?

The annual Social Security wage base cap is updated annually by the Social Security Administration ($176,100). Once an employee reaches this cumulative threshold in a calendar year, neither employee nor employer pays further Social Security tax on earnings above it.

When are employer payroll taxes due?

Tax deposits are made on either a monthly or semi-weekly schedule via EFTPS depending on your lookback liability. Quarterly returns (Form 941) are due by the end of the month following each calendar quarter (April 30, July 31, October 31, January 31).

What is the penalty for late payroll tax deposits?

The IRS Failure to Deposit penalty ranges from 2% (1-5 days late) to 10% (more than 15 days late), and rises to 15% if amounts remain unpaid after IRS demand notices. Using automated payroll software prevents late deposit penalties.
