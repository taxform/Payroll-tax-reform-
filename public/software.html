How to Choose Payroll Software

The best payroll software depends on your business size, employee locations, payroll frequency, and administrative needs.

1. Compare total costs

Look beyond the advertised monthly subscription. Check employee fees, additional state filing fees, implementation costs, and optional HR services.

2. Review payroll tax services

Some providers calculate payroll taxes but require employers to submit payments and returns themselves. Others offer tax filing and deposit services. Confirm exactly what each plan includes.

3. Consider accounting integration

Connecting payroll to accounting software can simplify expense tracking, reconciliation, and financial reporting.

4. Review customer support

Consider available support hours, payroll setup assistance, and access to help when tax filing deadlines approach.

5. Check multi-state capabilities

Employers with workers in multiple states should verify state tax registration, withholding, unemployment insurance, and payroll reporting support.

Payroll Software FAQ

What is payroll software?

Payroll software helps employers calculate wages, manage deductions, process employee payments, and prepare payroll records.

Does payroll software automatically file taxes?

Not always. Tax filing capabilities vary by provider and plan. Some services calculate taxes without submitting payments or returns.

Can small businesses use payroll software?

Yes. Payroll products are available for businesses with only a few employees as well as larger organizations.

Should I choose the cheapest payroll provider?

Price matters, but tax filing coverage, reliability, support, integrations, and total costs should also influence the decision.
