What your employer pays on top

Your salary isn’t the full cost of employing you. Employers match Social Security and Medicare, and pay federal (FUTA) and state (SUTA) unemployment tax on the first dollars you earn.

How this calculator does the math

It follows the same order a payroll system does. Small choices — like whether a deduction is pre-tax — change which taxes it lowers, so the order matters.

1

Start with gross, subtract pre-tax

Retirement contributions lower the wages taxed for federal income tax. Health and HSA amounts taken through a cafeteria plan lower both income-tax wages and FICA wages.

2

Apply FICA — Social Security & Medicare

Social Security is 6.2% on the first $184,500 of wages in 2026 (max $11,439). Medicare is 1.45% on every dollar, plus 0.9% extra on wages above $200,000 single / $250,000 joint.

3

Apply federal brackets, then credits

What’s left after pre-tax deductions and the standard deduction runs through the 2026 marginal brackets. Dependent amounts from Form W-4 then reduce the withholding estimate, and any extra withholding you entered is added per check.

Common questions

Why is my first paycheck with a new job sometimes taxed differently?

Payroll annualizes each check — it taxes a biweekly check as if you’ll earn that amount 26 times. A mid-year start, a bonus check, or a short first check can therefore be withheld at a rate that doesn’t match your final tax bill. The difference is settled when you file.

Does a 401(k) contribution lower Social Security and Medicare tax?

No. Traditional 401(k) and 403(b) contributions lower federal (and usually state) income-tax wages, but Social Security and Medicare are still calculated on your pay before the retirement contribution. Health premiums and HSA contributions through a cafeteria plan are different — they lower both.

When does Social Security tax stop during the year?

Once your 2026 wages pass $184,500, the 6.2% Social Security tax stops for the rest of the year — that’s a maximum of $11,439 from an employee. Medicare’s 1.45% never stops, and the extra 0.9% Medicare tax starts on wages above $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (joint) in this estimate.

What does my employer pay that I don’t see on my pay stub?

A matching 7.65% for Social Security and Medicare, plus unemployment taxes: FUTA at a net 0.6% on the first $7,000 of wages ($42 maximum per employee per year, after the usual state credit) and SUTA, which varies by state and employer. That’s why the calculator shows total employer cost separately.

I’m self-employed — why is the tax so much higher?

You pay both halves of FICA as self-employment tax: 15.3% total, applied to 92.35% of your net self-employment income. The silver lining is that half of that tax is deductible when figuring your federal income tax, which this calculator applies automatically in Self-Employed mode.

Is this my exact withholding?

No — it’s a careful estimate. Exact withholding depends on your full Form W-4, pay dates, year-to-date amounts, benefits, garnishments, and your state’s own tables. Use it to plan and to spot-check a pay stub, not as a substitute for your payroll provider or a tax professional.
