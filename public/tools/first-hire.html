Set it up right, once

Becoming an employer means new accounts, new filings, and new deadlines. Here is the full first-hire setup list, in the order to do it — then the payroll software that fits your situation, so the filings mostly take care of themselves.

First-hire setup checklist

  1. Get an EIN

    Apply for a free Employer Identification Number from the IRS. You need it before you can run payroll, open tax accounts, or hire. Don’t pay a third-party site for one — it’s free.

  2. Register as an employer with your state

    Open a state withholding account and a state unemployment (SUTA) account. Some states combine them into one registration. How SUTA works, state by state.

  3. Line up workers’ comp insurance

    Required for employers in nearly every state, often from the employee’s first day. Get quotes before the start date — rates depend heavily on the kind of work.

  4. Collect day-one paperwork

    Form W-4 (federal withholding), Form I-9 (work eligibility — complete by the first day, documents checked within 3 business days), your state’s withholding form, and a direct-deposit authorization if you’ll offer it.

  5. Report the new hire to your state

    Federal law requires reporting every new hire to your state’s registry within 20 days of the start date — some states give you less time. It’s a quick online form in most states.

  6. Enroll in EFTPS

    The free federal system for depositing payroll taxes. Enrollment credentials arrive by mail and can take about a week, so start this before your first payday, not after. How to pay federal payroll taxes with EFTPS.

  7. Know your deposit schedule

    The IRS assigns you a monthly or semiweekly deposit schedule based on your lookback-period tax liability. Brand-new employers with no history generally start as monthly depositors — but the schedule is not optional, and late deposits draw penalties fast.

  8. File Form 941 every quarter

    Reports wages paid, income tax withheld, and both shares of Social Security and Medicare. Due the last day of the month after each quarter ends. How to file Form 941.

  9. File Form 940 every year 2026 form due Feb 1, 2027

    The annual FUTA return. It’s normally due January 31, but January 31, 2027 falls on a Sunday — so the 2026 Form 940 is due Monday, February 1, 2027. How to file Form 940.

  10. Keep payroll records at least 4 years

    Employment tax records — returns, wage records, deposit receipts — must be kept for at least four years after the tax becomes due or is paid, whichever is later.
