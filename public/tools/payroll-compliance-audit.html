      The rules behind this audit

      Deposit schedule — the $50,000 lookbackLook at the four quarters ending June 30 of the prior year. Total Form 941 tax of $50,000 or less → monthly depositor (due the 15th of the following month). More than $50,000 → semiweekly: paydays Wed–Fri are due the following Wednesday; paydays Sat–Tue are due the following Friday.
      Failure-to-deposit tiers2% at 1–5 days late · 5% at 6–15 days · 10% at 16+ days (or not paid within 10 days of the first IRS notice) · 15% if still unpaid 10+ days after notice. Applied to the undeposited amount, per deposit.
      Filing & payment penaltiesFailure to file Form 941: 5% of net tax per month, up to 25%. Failure to pay: 0.5% per month, up to 25%. When both run in the same month, the filing penalty is reduced so the combined rate is 5% — this estimate keeps them separate, which makes it conservative on the high side.

      Disclaimer — read this before acting

      This audit is an educational self-check only. It is not legal advice, tax advice, or a compliance determination, and it does not create any professional relationship. Penalty figures are estimates built from published IRS rates and the numbers you entered; your actual liability depends on your deposit history, filing record, state, and facts this tool cannot see, and the IRS and states assess penalties case by case. Before acting on any finding — especially worker classification or unpaid trust fund taxes — consult a CPA or an employment attorney. For a binding answer on whether a worker is an employee or a contractor, you or the worker can file IRS Form SS-8 and let the IRS determine it.
